# HireMate — AI Mock Interview Platform

> Product strategy and launch case study · June 2026  
> **Status:** Portfolio capstone / product concept. Growth figures and KPI values below are proposed targets, not achieved results.

## 1. Product overview

HireMate is an AI-powered mock interview platform for students, graduates and early-career candidates. It is designed to help users practise role-specific interviews, receive structured feedback and understand what to improve before their next attempt.

**Product thesis:** the differentiator should not be another AI interviewer. It should be a reliable practice-to-improvement loop: simulate → evaluate → recommend → practise again.

## 2. Problem

Interview preparation is fragmented across generic question banks, videos, peer practice and expensive one-to-one coaching. Candidates can spend substantial time preparing without knowing:
- whether their answers are improving;
- which role-specific competencies remain weak;
- whether feedback is consistent and actionable; or
- what they should practise next.

The core product risk is feedback quality. A score without transparent criteria or evidence can reduce trust rather than improve readiness.

## 3. Target users

| Segment | Job to be done | Main need |
|---|---|---|
| Freshers and graduates | Prepare for placements and first roles | Affordable, repeatable practice and confidence |
| Lateral job seekers | Prepare for a role or company switch | JD-aligned practice and targeted feedback |
| MBA / career pivoters | Move into product, consulting or tech | Structured preparation and measurable progress |
| International candidates | Adapt to different interview formats | Role- and market-specific preparation |

**Initial wedge:** students and early-career candidates preparing for software, data, AI, product and business roles.

## 4. Proposed experience

1. **Set up:** choose target role, seniority, skills, interview type and difficulty.
2. **Simulate:** complete a behavioural, technical, case or role-specific mock interview.
3. **Evaluate:** receive rubric-based scores and evidence-backed feedback.
4. **Recommend:** turn weak competencies into a focused next-practice plan.
5. **Track:** review practice history and competency trends over time.

The evaluation experience should explain what worked, what was weak and why. Scores should use stable criteria across attempts so users can interpret progress meaningfully.

## 5. MVP scope and prioritisation

| Feature | Priority | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| AI mock interview session | P0 | Core value proposition |
| Role-based question bank | P0 | Tests whether role specificity matters |
| AI feedback summary | P0 | Tests whether feedback quality drives repeat use |
| User account and session history | P1 | Enables progress tracking and retention |
| Waitlist / referral module | P1 | Supports early acquisition |

**MVP principle:** build the smallest product that tests the riskiest assumption — that useful, trustworthy AI feedback makes candidates return and practise again.

## 6. Business model hypothesis

The launch plan proposes a freemium model:

| Tier | Proposed offer | Strategic role |
|---|---|---|
| Free | 3 sessions/month, basic feedback, limited roles | Acquisition and activation |
| Pro — ₹499/month | More sessions, detailed feedback, broader role coverage, benchmark score and growth plan | Core monetisation |
| Premium — ₹999/month | Pro plus proposed live coaching, resume / LinkedIn review and priority support | Higher-value segment |

These are proposed packaging and pricing assumptions. They require willingness-to-pay research and validation before launch.

## 7. Go-to-market plan

| Phase | User target | Focus |
|---|---:|---|
| Months 1–2 | 0 → 1,000 | Waitlist, college partnerships, referral loop and early-adopter community |
| Months 3–4 | 1,000 → 5,000 | User stories, content, targeted acquisition and email nurture |
| Months 5–6 | 5,000 → 10,000 | Proven channel scaling, creator partnerships and institutional pilots |

Potential growth loops include shareable benchmark scorecards, referral rewards, interview-preparation content and college placement-cell partnerships.

## 8. Success metrics

**North Star:** Weekly Active Interviewers (WAI) — unique users completing at least one mock interview in a week.

| Metric | Proposed target |
|---|---:|
| Sessions per active user | ≥ 2/week |
| Session completion rate | > 75% |
| Feedback review rate | > 60% |
| Free-to-paid conversion (30 days) | 8–12% |
| D7 retention | > 40% |
| D30 retention | > 20% |
| Referral rate | > 15% |
| LTV:CAC | > 3:1 |

These are planning targets from the launch strategy, not measured product performance.

## 9. Key risks and validation priorities

- **Evaluation trust:** validate rubric consistency, evidence quality and score stability across repeated attempts.
- **Feedback usefulness:** test whether users understand and act on the next-practice recommendation.
- **Retention:** measure repeat sessions and feedback review, not just sign-ups.
- **AI reliability:** monitor unsupported feedback, hallucinations and variation across equivalent answers.
- **Willingness to pay:** test pricing with target segments before treating proposed tiers as validated.

## 10. What I would validate first

Run moderated usability sessions with students and early-career candidates. Ask participants to complete one role-specific interview, interpret the feedback and choose their next practice action. Evaluate rubric comprehension, perceived fairness, actionability and intent to return.

## Project classification

This document describes product strategy, MVP scope and a proposed go-to-market plan. It does not claim 10,000 users, achieved retention, revenue, CAC or a completed live experiment.
